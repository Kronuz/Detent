# Authority readiness

This is the next component in the high-effort Architect's Validated Architectural Blueprint. It follows Linux completion qualification and precedes portable local views and fleet distribution. Implementation is in progress; the checks below are acceptance gates rather than claims of production readiness. Review runs once after the full component, with architectural questions handled during implementation.

## Checkpoint ownership and bounded work

Keep the existing materializing Core APIs and checkpoint formats. The Worker path captures a frozen capability containing the owning Core, persistence token, receiver configuration and hard state, application boundary, and retained range. Each synchronous entry access checks that the exact pending publication still owns the range. Asynchronous IO owns its encoded bytes. Reliable Applied completion remains serviceable during capture; incoming activation keeps its existing barrier.

Encode incrementally with separate byte and record budgets, including partial scalar headers, large payloads and empty NoOp entries. Capture the receiver's durable storage sequence once after cutover admission closes. Reserve the entire replacement before capture, retain at most one encoded chunk through original write completion, and fence an encoding error after Core accepts cutover. Cancellation before cutover disposes known staging; cancellation afterward cannot erase original publication ownership.

Log prefix compaction and conflicting suffix replacement must detach logical ranges without destroying all their payloads in that owner turn. Charge both live and retired nodes and bytes. Reclaim with entry and byte budgets, retain control capacity under pressure, and avoid moving the destruction pause into cancellation, the next append, or an obsolete capture's release. Tree balancing and capacity checks must precede visible mutation; deterministic priorities alone do not provide an adversarial balance guarantee.

The original synchronous Worker benchmark is recorded in the cluster scratch measurement: 64, 4096 and 65535 retained 1 KiB commands, a 1 MiB application image, and the same compiler and filesystem for comparison. The largest case encodes 68484227 bytes and has a longest frozen owner turn of 92.264 ms CPU and 93.1759 ms wall, with process peak RSS of 296710144 bytes. These are individual local samples, not production tails. Measure admission debt, Core freeze, encoding, publication and original IO wait separately; bounded CPU work alone does not shorten a slow durability freeze.

## Connection admission before dispatch

The TLS factory's existing connection lease starts on the destination executor. A blocked destination can therefore accumulate accepted sockets and posted session controls before that lease exists. Add an optional transport-neutral gate to reactor's TCP server and expose it compatibly through HTTP. Acquire a move-only lease on the accepting executor before posting the session, and retain it through queued launch, handshake, serving, original IO and session retirement. Refund failed launch and shutdown exactly once.

The authority uses an explicit global bound. Preserve existing constructor behavior for other consumers. Handshake capacity remains separate; queued connections count against total capacity, acceptance timestamps make queue wait consume the handshake deadline, and the documented bound includes the finite accepts already in progress. Reject excess sockets before SSL allocation. Qualify both shared-acceptor and reuse-port modes with a deliberately blocked destination.

## Bounded observations

Add fixed counters and histograms with primitive, outcome and configured-voter dimensions only. Retain timestamps in existing bounded flights and requests rather than introducing an unbounded correlation map. Counters saturate, units are explicit, and timeouts and obsolete outcomes remain separate from successful latency samples.

Measure primitive submission to original reaping, owned-job admission to settlement, frame decoding to Worker admission, correlated request emission to response decoding, publisher admission to result readiness, result readiness to original reply settlement, owner turns and scheduled wakeup lateness, and checkpoint phases. Submission-to-reaping includes observation and scheduling; it is not device latency. Report occupancy and high-water marks for connections, peer queues, publisher credits, retired logs and outstanding IO.

Publish a health snapshot about every 250 ms and immediately on readiness or fencing. Serialize outside the service critical section, preserve existing fields, and expose snapshot age. If committed application work exceeds the owner budget, introduce one bounded application slot at durable entry boundaries while keeping transactions atomic and the Applied obligation reliable.

## Combined qualification

Require independent byte fixtures for both checkpoint formats, maximum commands, empty histories, many empty entries, large prefix retirement and conflicting incoming snapshots. Compare the 64/4096/65535-entry benchmark before and after, including peak retained memory, maximum owner work, process CPU, wall time and freeze. Ordinary qualified owner turns target less than one tenth of the heartbeat interval; report operating-system scheduling stalls separately.

Exercise actual three-process verified HTTPS with minority isolation during publishing, nonempty snapshot catch-up, disconnect after manifest publication, lost replies, leadership replacement with pending writes and restart. Match acknowledged identities and recovered results, and require a bounded strong-read failure without quorum.

Drive hundreds of distinct producers below, near and above the measured plateau, with slow request bodies, stalled readers, handshake floods and snapshots. Report offered, admitted, committed, rejected and unknown outcomes separately from client delay. After pressure drains, verify session, reply, credit, retired-root and IO ownership returns to its steady state. Capacity rejection is retryable and consumes no producer sequence before consensus admission; admitted requests with lost replies retain an unknown outcome.

Run shared cluster, reactor, HTTP and Detent native and sanitizer regressions. Preserve legacy public APIs, journal formats and Xapiand's cluster wire behavior. Measure telemetry overhead at a stated workload rate. Virtual-machine process CPU does not justify a fleet power or cost claim.

FreeBSD 14.4-RELEASE-p10 passed all 45 Detent tests in 128.21 seconds with base Clang/libc++ 19.1.7, the explicit experimental-library flag described in [Build](../README.md#build), and Bash installed for the shell fixtures. This includes native completion storage, HTTPS, pressure, multi-voter failover and snapshot catch-up. The run covers the readiness working tree before the finer command continuations; FreeBSD sanitizer and large retained-prefix benchmark qualification remain open. It establishes functional behavior in the personal UFS virtual machine, not fleet performance or device power-loss guarantees.

## Bounded health observations

The current implementation publishes cached health about every 250 ms, initially before service readiness, and immediately on fencing. `snapshot_age_ms` reports cache age when read. Transport counters use a shared admission observer whose lifetime is independent of the HTTP server. Snapshot serialization runs outside the service and accounting locks.

Duration fields use inclusive nanosecond bounds in `duration_bucket_upper_bounds_ns`; the final bucket covers larger values. Each histogram exposes count, saturating sum and maximum. Atomic histogram fields are sampled independently, so a concurrent sample is approximate rather than an atomic transaction.

`io_submitted_to_reaped_ns` and its failed counterpart measure original primitive submission through observed completion, including queueing and scheduling. `io_original_drive_ns` ends when the driver returns; `io_job_settlement_ns` ends when the Worker releases the completed operation after settling its storage state. `io_job_fence_observed_ns` records completed originals observed after fencing separately. These measurements do not isolate device latency.

`owner_turn_ns` measures the authority scheduling pass, including health serialization, and excludes its timer wait. It does not include separately resumed driver or peer coroutines. `wake_lateness_ns` measures resumption after the scheduled timer deadline. `peer_decoded_to_admitted_ns` ends when a retained decoded control event enters the Worker.

`publisher_admitted_to_ready_ns` covers admitted tasks through their completion callback, including body intake and outcome failures. Immediate validation errors that never become tasks are excluded. `reply_ready_to_retired_ns` covers a deferred reply lease through retirement, including rejected or abandoned replies; capability retirement is not proof of delivery. Neither field is a success-only latency metric. Additional outcome and request-correlated replication measurements remain part of component qualification.

The [histogram microbenchmark](../benchmarks/metrics-footprint-baseline.json) records three uncontended one-thread repeats of one million observations with AppleClang 17 Release on the Intel i9/macOS host. Median control-adjusted CPU costs are 4.93 ns for owner counters, 15.85 ns for atomic counters, and 80.68 ns for owner counters with two clock samples. Each histogram occupies 144 bytes; the enabled completion driver owns 25 histograms, totaling 3600 bytes of histogram storage. Disabled driver collection allocates none of these histograms and skips its clock reads.

At 100000 observations per second, the two-clock microbenchmark cost corresponds to about 0.0081 CPU cores. This is an arithmetic projection of instrumentation observations, not requests or fleet deployment cost. It excludes health serialization, contention, allocator overhead and surrounding protocol work. The end-to-end telemetry comparison remains open.

## Application scheduling correction

The Linux virtual-machine pressure fixture exceeds the owner CPU target while applying whole committed ranges. Node retains one existing bounded `Committed` range and advances at most one entry per `service()` turn. Command entries decode and apply into one unpublished candidate across turns, with budgets of eight commands, 128 mutations and 64 KiB of encoded command bytes. One indivisible command that exceeds a turn budget may run on a fresh turn, subject to the replicated semantic profile. Repeated `drain()` calls from proposal and read admission do not advance application work. Entries publish their state and receipts atomically; processed payloads retire individually, and the original range receives exactly one final `Applied` acknowledgement. A middle-entry failure reports the original range endpoint so Worker accepts the failure.

Transport, storage and maintenance continue while the application slot is occupied, including when host network output is retained. Local checkpoint capture waits for the slot to drain, and snapshot activation explicitly requires an empty slot. A quorum-ready read must have reached its application boundary. Role loss can discard a caller's interest without discarding committed work.

The synchronous 100k-knob benchmark measured 73.234 ms of CPU in one service turn before command quanta. The candidate preserves ordered batch semantics, including semantic rejections, and publishes state and receipts only after exact payload exhaustion. A malformed later command discards the candidate and fences the original range. Command quanta bound work units, not CPU or wall time: one configured maximum command, garbage collection and final publication still require measurement. Maximum-command qualification and the repeated pressure comparison remain open. Owner health now samples thread CPU separately from wall time, with an explicit counter for unsupported or failed CPU-clock observations.

The isolated [100k-knob comparison](../benchmarks/application-quanta-baseline.json) measures a maximum load service CPU turn of 73.234 ms before command quanta and 3.721 ms afterward. Total load CPU is 1198.843 versus 1189.425 ms, so this sample shows work spread across turns rather than a meaningful total CPU reduction. Maximum wall turns remain dominated by synchronous disk waits in this fixture (73.437 versus 78.929 ms). These are single samples, not production tails or a maximum-command guarantee.

Append transport timing retains one observation per authenticated control connection. The clock starts immediately before the original write attempt, excluding queue residence, and settles at authenticated response decoding independently of Worker admission. RPC and read-probe identity must match; higher terms have a separate correlated-reply histogram and still reach Core. Lower terms and unmatched identifiers never settle an observation. Superseded records and observation deadlines are transport outcomes, not proof of Core obsolescence or timeout. Write failure and disconnect retire only their current observation; a response that arrived before the original write resumed cannot be counted twice. Votes remain excluded because their wire format has no RPC identity.

Node already initiated checkpoints at half the configured retained-entry or byte limit. The policy now latches due state, requires a compactable prefix, and retries an eligible application boundary before draining the next delivered range. A failed replacement reservation retains due state and retries after 100 logical milliseconds. Only actual published base advancement clears the latch, and existing Worker scheduling continues to own preparation, original IO, publication and reclamation. Role changes do not cancel healthy local preparation. Qualification of sustained backlog and real nonempty three-process snapshot catch-up remains in progress.

Attribution of capture starvation uses the same 32-entry replay fixture, four-entry delivery ranges and no election-time advance. Linking the pre-policy Node (`6d667cf`) into that fixture fails its `early_capture` assertion. The updated Node passes both synchronous and owned-completion variants in 8.34 seconds, observing capture before the backlog finishes and published base advancement afterward. The earlier election-enabled fixture was insufficient because persistence itself created a capture opportunity.

The [native completion checkpoint qualification](../benchmarks/native-checkpoint-readiness.json) observes every dispatched Worker and original driver callback, with telemetry enabled, two checkpoint publications and full physical prefix retirement. Three isolated macOS samples at 65535 retained 1 KiB commands measure maximum callback CPU between 0.953 and 1.429 ms, maximum callback wall between 0.964 and 1.798 ms, and process peak RSS between 126750720 and 127709184 bytes. The interval lasts 6.414–6.539 seconds, including original IO and both publications. This meets the 2 ms ordinary callback target in these samples; it does not guarantee scheduling tails or application-command CPU. The freeze-to-retirement field includes the second preparation and publication rather than measuring one individual freeze.

## Resumable read responses

The read component uses one frozen capture across exact counting, reply quota reservation and incremental JSON encoding. Completed quorum reads retain their admitted task and verified boundary without creating more captures. Authority metadata is encoded directly into the body, and the counted reservation transfers to original HTTP egress settlement. The completion-delivery cursor invokes at most eight callbacks per turn; expiration and terminal settlement have separate bounded cursors.

An older snapshot capture explicitly retires and collects its arena before release; current-arena cleanup transfers to Node. Native macOS, Linux and FreeBSD gates and macOS ASan/UBSan cover stalled egress credit, deadline cancellation, mixed read/write progress and a capture spanning actual follower snapshot activation. The codec's independent oracle also checks exact JSON, escaping and a 32768-override read across fresh-arena replacement. Production transport overlap and the remaining terminal gates below remain open.

The [paired measurements](../benchmarks/read-response-measurements.json) include the scheduler correction: unused Node allocation is reused, and frontend alternation applies to runnable jobs. Large replies take more wall time and total owner CPU than synchronous serialization, while the measured maximum stays below 0.278 ms. These histograms are sampled before Service destruction and exclude final live State/Core teardown. Terminal retirement is measured separately.

### Terminal ownership retirement

Shutdown now closes Node/Worker admission before retiring application ownership. Worker drives only already-created storage operations, observes their original outcomes, and starts no new persistence or maintenance operations. Prepublication replacements settle as known staging debt; accepted publication finishes its original barriers and may leave activation to recovery. Core then invalidates frozen capabilities, detaches live log roots, and incrementally retires payload-bearing actions and entries. Node discards unpublished command candidates, retains old snapshot arenas through collection, retires its final State roots, and drains local obligations before the owner is destroyed. SnapshotChannel rejects late sessions and traffic after close. These additive APIs leave legacy Raft behavior and journal formats unchanged.

This implementation is under qualification. Native and ASan/UBSan Core tests cover capture invalidation, suppressed post-close events and bounded retirement with a maximum legal payload. The 87 Worker terminal checks hold initialization, preparation and publication originals, verify no premature ownership release or refund, reopen exact published bytes, suppress retained output after close, and exercise safe abandonment before submission. They pass on macOS, Linux and FreeBSD, with macOS ASan/UBSan. Five Node/Service/cluster gates pass on all three platforms and under macOS sanitizers; the cluster fixture pauses a read capture through actual follower snapshot activation and explicitly collects its old arena. All 14 native cluster tests and all 46 native Detent tests pass before the subsequent retirement-budget adjustment. The expanded 167-check terminal gate also holds accepted snapshot verification opens, reads and reclamation originals, then shuts down after actual publication but before activation; delayed activation success and failure callbacks cannot revive or taint the closed worker. An additional 277-check source gate exercises actual SnapshotChannel disconnect with accepted source verification reads before and after close. Both expanded Worker gates pass on macOS, Linux and FreeBSD and under macOS ASan/UBSan. Updated Node/Service/cluster gates, including the shutdown backlog regression, pass on all three supported platforms and under macOS ASan/UBSan. Representative pressure/churn measurements, whole-service telemetry overhead remain open; the [consolidated Architect review](AUTHORITY_COMPONENT_REVIEW.md) gives source GO. Previously recorded read-response owner histograms exclude final Service destruction and cannot qualify this terminal path.

The [terminal memory measurements](../benchmarks/terminal-retirement-measurements.json) compare current synchronous destructor fallbacks with posted retirement of 500000 live integer definitions and a separate strict Core containing 65536 entries and 64 MiB of payload. Synchronous reset occupies 234–247 ms. Initial posted runs permit unrelated callbacks between all 31259 retirement turns, but one callback measures 2.318 ms, exceeding the 2 ms target. A separate phase diagnostic measures 1.086 ms in log retirement and 0.008 ms in state collection; it does not identify the phase of the earlier 2.318 ms sample. Worker now limits terminal retirement to 32 units per turn while preserving room for a maximum legal entry. Three updated runs, including callback scheduling and phase-clock overhead, measure maximum callbacks of 0.971–1.056 ms and final destruction of 4.12–4.70 µs; all 31259 unrelated callbacks execute between retirement turns. The initial over-target sample remains in the artifact. These fixtures isolate memory ownership and exclude network/disk shutdown. Total retirement CPU remains comparable, and no efficiency or power saving is claimed.

Driver failure closes admission and retains protocol containers for budgeted retirement. A host reporting driver termination may abandon an operation locally only when no submitted original remains and the backend no longer owns it; this does not synthesize a completion. A live original remains owned until its actual reaping. If a driver exits while its original remains in flight, the authority follows the backend's existing fail-stop policy rather than refunding admission or leaving a dead actor waiting forever. Normal syscall failures arrive through their authentic operation result. Late application callbacks and `take_actions()` cannot restart work after terminal close.

Queued read tasks retain ingress admission through budgeted key-list disposal as well as original egress settlement. Terminal map and deque cursors remove entries as they complete each bounded callback slice, avoiding a bulk release at coroutine destruction. The service regression holds sixteen maximum-default-size key lists behind reply pressure, destroys the owner, then verifies terminal replies and eventual admission release. The regression passes on macOS, Linux and FreeBSD, with macOS ASan/UBSan.
