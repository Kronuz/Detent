## Verdict

**APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS: the shape holds and option (a) is safe to land, after the live record drops the storage identity and the scrub stops re-reporting old damage.**

What holds, and reproduces:

- Crash order today. `.live` is one more child of the one `PublicationOperation`: every child name is inspected as absent (Preflight), every child is prepared, then `selected` is replaced and the directory synced (`disk_store.cc:560-610`, Replace at `:584`, the directory sync at `:606`, phases at `:658`). No crash point leaves a durable `selected` reaching a generation without its `.live`, and an interrupted publication leaves only unreached names, which the collector inspects and removes with the generation (`disk_store.cc:947-956`, part 4 before the lease).
- The trust boundary. Only a coordinator recovery with no admission and no prior trusts (`generation.cc:358`, `disk_store.cc:1813`); `recover_generation` and every recovery after a publication take the walk or the warm path as before. `.read` and `.live` are written by one function only (`paged_publication_artifacts.cc:79-97`).
- `.live` agrees with the walk for every plan kind I could drive: snapshot (the suite's oracle at three revisions), and compaction and delta (P3: 10 publications, 0 mismatches in rows, counts or histogram).
- A snapshot publication shares no pack with its base (P2: 12 of 12 packs new at both revisions, 0 shared, even when one knob changes). Replacement plans have no registry (`paged_plan.cc:66-94`, `registry_` is set only by the delta and maintenance constructors), so `populate` writes no reused row for them (`paged_publication_artifacts.cc:42-54`), and the encoder takes the snapshot alone (`paged_snapshot_plan.cc:19`).
- First touch reaches the maintenance equality walk too (`paged_cursor.cc:88`), so no plan reads a base page unchecked.
- T5, the refusals and the oracle reproduce under ASan and UBSan at the full store (numbers above), with the same outcomes the ledger records.

What blocks: L1 (the storage identity in `.live`, finding A1) and L2 (the scrub's failure list, finding A6).

## Findings

**A1. The live record freezes the storage identity that D8 removes from every generation file.** Bytes 16-31 hold the host's storage identity (`live_record.hh:22`, `live_record.cc:34,65`), the decoder requires it nonzero (`live_record.cc:28,69`), and the restart requires it equal to `storage.meta` (`trusted_restart.cc:62`). D8.1 and W11 take that identity out of `.read`, `.owner` and `.lease` so two builds of the same input are byte-identical (T6), D9 seeds stores that have no `storage.meta` (adoption writes a fresh one last), and D7's relay ships a prepared generation in the agent's layout. With the field, an image's `.live` either carries a zero identity, which the decoder refuses, or the builder's, which the adopting agent's `storage.meta` never matches: every seeded store would be refused at its first trusted start, or S9 would have to rewrite a seeded file it is meant to open read-only. The field binds nothing today: `.live` already binds `.read`'s own digest (`trusted_restart.cc:63-65`), and `.read` carries the storage identity, checked at `trusted_restart.cc:52`. Fix now, while the format is pre-release: drop the field (bytes 16-31 become reserved and zero, or the header shrinks to 1136 bytes), keep version 1, and update `paged_publication_artifacts.cc:84`, `trusted_records.hh`, the fuzz driver's seed and the table in `docs/mechanisms/trusted-restart.md`. Nothing else in the record is host-specific: the generation id, root checksum and rows are functions of the build, so the record becomes byte-identical across hosts once S7 derives the ids. The rest of the format carries what S7 needs: the build ordinal enters through the generation id (D8.1), and the rows (id, length, pack SHA-256) are what S7 needs to open packs on first use and skip the per-segment `.owner` read that keeps today's restart O(segments) (ledger, "Design vs code").

**A2. The design does not know about `.live`, and S7 builds from the design.** D8's file list (`namespace-delivery.md:220`), W9, W11 and D8.4's list of names a publication verifies on reuse ("the owner record's checksum for a pack, the table's digests for a root or `.read`") do not include `.live`, and W12 still asks for a manifest digest in `.read` that shape A replaces. The inversion is sound: the rows and counts are functions of the Merkle root, so the root checksum fixes them, and `.read`'s digest fixes the file set. But S7 would build a second manifest (two ways to do one thing) and would leave `.live` outside D8.4 (A3). Fix: fold shape A into the design (D8's list, W9, W11, W12 satisfied by `.live` with `.read` unchanged, D10.2's manifest named as `.live`), and record the binding direction (`.live` binds `.read`, the table binds `.read`). A design edit for the lead (condition C1), not this branch.

**A3. The crash point is S7's, not today's: a reused `.live` name that D8.4 does not verify.** With random ids (C2) no two builds share a name, so today has no such point (above). Under D8.1, two different builds from the same base at the same position derive the same generation id and segment names: a compaction with a different selection after a crash, or a compaction and a `Republish` or maintenance build of one base, since the id omits the plan kind. A crash after the first build's files are linked leaves its `.live` under the final name. D8.4 handles a mismatching pack, root or `.read` (unlink it and link the new one, N1). For `.live` it says nothing, so the second build either wedges on `linkat`'s `EEXIST` (the 162-of-643 wedge class N1 measured) or keeps the stale `.live`, whose `.read` digest names the first build's `.read`. Once `selected` is replaced, the next cold start refuses a store that is fine (`trusted_restart.cc:63-65`). The other direction, a stale `.live` accepted against the new packs, needs the same `.read` digest and so the same root, which fixes the same rows: none found. Fix for S7 (C1): `.live` joins D8.4 with the publication's own bytes as the expected digest, "reached by the table" through its generation entry, and T7's crash matrix includes it.

**A4. The trust split is right; the words about it overstate the verification after a publication.** The ledger and the mechanism note say the recovery after a publication "still walks the new generation" (`trusted-restart.md`, Limits; ledger, "Design vs code"). After a delta over a trusted base that recovery is warm (`generation.cc:345,408`): it validates the fresh segments and changed pages and carries the prior's mappings with their first-touch bitmaps, so a trusted pack that a delta reuses is hashed by nothing but the scrub. That is correct (S4 makes it the rule), but the sentence should say "walks or warm-validates, as before S3". The proof of A1's claim, "`.live` equals the walk", covered snapshot plans only: `GenerationWriter` builds `PagedSnapshotPlanBuilder` (`generation_writer.cc:185`), whose plans write no reused row, and the fixtures overwrite the publication's `.live` with a walk-derived one for hand-installed bases (`trusted_records.hh`). P3 closes the gap for delta and compaction, but over 1- and 2-pack stores; the delta finalizer's arithmetic (`sparse_plan_finalizer.cc:116-123`, base live minus proven removals) is the same one warm recovery used before S3. `paged_disk_fixture.hh:35-38` swallows any failure of `derive`, so a fixture that fails to write its records surfaces later as an unrelated refusal; it should let the error out or name it (C3).

**A5. The "republish a missing `.read`" branch is safe to remove.** `metadata_ready` returns false only on `ENOENT` (`prepared_reader.cc:82-83`), every paged publication writes `.read` (`paged_publication_artifacts.cc:97`), and a cold start without `.read` is refused before the probe runs. So the branch at `coordinator.cc:362-372` runs only if something else unlinks `.read` between the trusted open and the probe in the same worker task. With the branch gone, that case falls through to `PagedMaintenanceBuilder`, which re-encodes the base into a new generation through first touch: safe, but by accident. Remove it together with `PagedPlanBuilder::Republish`, `PagedPlanState::Republish` and their tests (`test_prepared_republish.cc`, the matching `test_prepared_startup.cc` case), and make `migrate_flat_head` (`coordinator.cc:531`) act only on a layout-1 base, so a paged base without `.read` is an error. This is internal and changes no user-visible behavior. The lead decides whether it lands here or next.

**A6. The scrub re-reports old damage, so the operator's signal under (a) is wrong.** `PackScrub::begin` never clears `failures_` (`pack_scrub.cc:12-21`, appended at `:53`). P1: one damaged pack gives `failures().size()` 1, 2, 3 over three passes, and a clean pack set started after it still reports 3 before and after its full pass. The coordinator resets its own count on a new root (`coordinator.cc:655`) and compares sizes (`:662-663`). So (1) `damaged_packs` counts damage once per pass, not packs, and `diagnostic.failures` grows by one every pass (about one every 50 minutes for 1.51 GB at the default 512 KiB/s); (2) after a fresh generation replaces a damaged one in the same process (the snapshot path T5 exercises), the agent immediately reports the old count as damage in the clean generation; (3) the list grows without bound. Under (a) this report is the only signal that tells an operator to bootstrap, so it has to be exact. Fix: clear `failures_` in `begin`, define `failures()` as the last completed pass's packs, report `damaged_packs` from that, count one coordinator failure per pass that finds damage, and add P1's three cases to `test_pack_scrub.cc` (L2).

**A7. Option (a) is safe to land. The premise for (b) is wrong, and (b) is smaller than stated.**

1. Values. Under (a) no read returns a damaged byte. The root is hashed at every start, every page is checked against its parent's reference on first touch, the bit lives on the mapping and survives `append`, and every plan's base reads go through it, including the maintenance equality walk (`paged_cursor.cc:88`). The one exception is the documented limit: a page verified before it is damaged stays verified for the mapping's life. Not refusing a store whose pack is damaged does not make readers worse off. They map `selected` themselves with their own first touch, and they see the same generation whether or not the agent is up. A refusal would only stop the agent from delivering updates that never touch the damage.
2. Reuse. A delta or compaction reuses a base pack by reference without reading its untouched pages (`paged_publication_artifacts.cc:46-54`; the `Republish` plan reuses every pack), so damage is carried forward by reference, still caught on first touch and still reported by the scrub. A snapshot never reuses a pack (P2), so neither `paged_reuse.cc` nor `paged_contextual_reuse.cc`, which serve delta and compaction plans, can carry damage into a Replace. Option (b) needs no `excluded_segments` input.
3. Liveness under (a). A delta whose path crosses a damaged page fails first touch while it builds. The candidate loop counts the failure and retries the same base (`coordinator.cc:1033-1060`). Nothing sets `force_snapshot` (only `HistoryLost` and `RepresentationLimit` do, `coordinator.cc:942`), so every later delta that changes a key under that page fails too. The agent then stays at the old revision for every namespace (one generation today), while the readers keep serving a valid but stale revision. A Replace at a new cursor heals it by accident: the publication is durable and clean, the warm recovery after it reads the damaged prior and fails, `settle_failure` fences the store (`disk_store.cc:1821-1839`), and the restart trusts the clean generation (the ledger's T5 note). A compaction that picks a damaged pack fails each `compaction_interval` and costs that round (`coordinator.cc:561-569` catches only pressure, allocation and length errors).
4. What (b) needs on today's store: request a Replace on a scrub failure or a `ReadSourceFailure`; plan it with an intent that skips the maintenance equality, because at an unchanged cursor that walk throws on the damaged page or finds the state unchanged and publishes nothing (`paged_snapshot_plan.cc:74-83`); and recover after it without the damaged prior. Random ids already give the repair fresh names. S7 adds only "ordinal r + 1" to the id, so the coordinator's repair state is kept at S7, not rebuilt, and the ledger's "(b) would be rebuilt there" overstates the cost.

Ruling on the question put to me: (a) is safe to land, with A6 fixed and the operator's path written down (C2). Whether (b) is built before S7 is the lead's call; with these three pieces it is a contained step, not a dependency on ordinals.

**A8. "Surface in health" is not built.** The lead ruled that damaged packs "surface in status and health". The branch fills `CoordinatorStatus::scrub` and logs a warning (`managed_host.cc:72-76`). The agent's `health()` is the client's (`agent.hh:55`), which knows nothing of the scrub. Under (a) that leaves health reporting healthy while the scrub finds damage. Either wire `scrub.source_failure` into the health the agent reports, or the lead narrows the ruling (C2).

**A9. A store written before S3 is refused, and the refusal is fatal.** Every existing store has no `.live`. The cold start refuses it, the startup recovery's exception ends the coordinator (`coordinator.cc:862`, `fatal` at `:1137-1140`), and the operator must bootstrap. That is the lead's ruling ("no second restart path") and it is acceptable before release, but `CHANGELOG.md` does not say it. Condition C2.

## Blocking items

- L1 (A1): remove the storage identity from the live record's format, encoder, decoder and restart check; update the mechanism note's table, `trusted_records.hh` and the fuzz seed; the refusal test "live record swapped from an older generation" still refuses.
- L2 (A6): `PackScrub::begin` clears the failure list; `failures()` is the last completed pass's damaged packs; the coordinator reports packs, not cumulative entries, and one failure per damaged pass; `test_pack_scrub.cc` covers two passes over one damaged pack (1, not 2) and a clean set after a damaged one (0).

## Conditions

- C1 (A2, A3), before S7 starts: the design records shape A (D8's file list, W9, W11, W12 satisfied by `.live`, D10.2's manifest named as `.live`, the binding direction), D8.4 verifies a reused `.live` against the publication's own bytes, and T7's crash matrix includes it.
- C2 (A7, A8, A9), before the gate: the ledger and the mechanism note state the operator's repair under (a) (scrub warning or stuck deltas, then bootstrap a fresh store), the stuck-delta behavior and the fence-then-restart heal; health reflects `scrub.source_failure`, or the lead narrows the ruling; `CHANGELOG.md` says a pre-S3 store is refused and must be bootstrapped.
- C3 (A4): the "recovery after a publication" wording says warm or walked; `paged_disk_fixture.hh` stops swallowing `derive` failures; the oracle gains a multi-pack delta and compaction over the 100K store, so the reused-row branch of `populate` is checked against the walk at scale (P3 is the template).
