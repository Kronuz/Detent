# Paged transfer

A transfer ships the dense pages a receiver lacks, and the receiver verifies and writes them instead of building. `src/detent_local/paged_transfer.{hh,cc}` implements [dense-distribution](../design/dense-distribution.md) D2 to D4; the [model](../design/dense-distribution-model.py) is its oracle.

## The transfer

`make_transfer(pages, read, limits)` takes the target's pages that the receiver lacks, in the target's seal order (every child before its parent, the root last), and returns them back to back: no padding, no segment table. Each page is the stored page with three fields rewritten (D3):

1. The header digest [32, 64) is zero.
2. A child that is in the transfer has a zero content digest, and its placement entry holds 12 zero bytes, its segment number from 1 and its offset in the derived layout.
3. Any other child is a base child: it keeps its content digest, and its placement entry is zero.

Keys, length and height stay in every content entry. The derived layout is `transfer_layout`, the build's rule: each page at the next 8-byte offset, in a new segment of at most 1 MiB when it does not fit. A full copy is the transfer of every page of a generation; a delta, the transfer of the target's pages whose summary differs from the base page at the same trie path.

## The receiver's checks, in order

`receive_transfer(account, stream, base, target, segment_id, limits)` takes the stream, the base generation (null for the empty base), and what the frame names: the root digest, the position's revision and the namespace. It returns the segments to write, numbered from 1 with ids from `segment_id`, each with its pack checksum, and the root's reference. It throws `TransferRefused`, whose `reason()` names the check:

1. **Split and lay out** (`Framing`, `Limits`). Every page starts with `DTPAGE03` and a length of 79 to 65,536 that ends inside the stream; pages, bytes and segments stay within the limits. The pages are copied into their derived segments. An empty stream is the empty state when there is no base and the frame names the empty commitment, or the base itself when the frame names the base's root; any other empty stream is refused (`Empty`).
2. **Headers** (`Page`). Every header decodes as dense-pages' `decode_page_header` decodes it.
3. **Walk from the root** (`Layout`, `ParentFirst`, `Twice`, `BaseLacks`, `Page`). From the last page, in trie order, through `NodeAt` steps that must follow preorder: each external slot names the next content entry; a nonzero placement must name a page start of the derived layout, an earlier page, referenced once; a zero placement resolves the base page at the slot's trie path, walking the base from its root by prefixes and labels. The walk emits D5's seal order: after a node's subtrees, the transfer children it seals in `seals_before` order, and a page over 4,096 bytes after its own top node.
4. **References and order** (`Unreferenced`, `Order`). Every page but the last is referenced, and the transfer's order equals the seal order the walk emitted.
5. **Hash bottom-up**, in transfer order (`ContentDigest`, `ContentSummary`, `PageDigest`, `Page`, `Leaf`). Each content entry's keys, length and height equal its child's; a stated content digest is zero or the child's; the receiver writes the child's digest and its placement (its own segment id and offset, or the base page's). It hashes the page once (`page_commitment`), compares a stated header digest, writes the digest, then runs dense-pages' `PageValidator` with digests not compared: preorder structure, node count, labels, keys, height and D5's sealing at every node. Every leaf is checked as a first touch checks it: a definition by `validate_definition` at the frame's revision, a watermark at most that revision. Every leaf's key is the frame's namespace's (`'K' name 0 local 0`, or its watermark `'W' name 0`), and a full copy holds exactly one watermark (`Leaf`); a target with an empty namespace binds none, for the model's multi-namespace corpora.
6. **The root** (`RootDigest`). The last page's digest equals the frame's. Then each segment is hashed once for its pack checksum.

Placement is outside the page digest, so the layout and order checks (1, 3, 4) are what make the receiver's files a function of the target's pages alone: a sender cannot choose segment breaks or page order. Steps 3 and 5 are the defense against a wrong tree under a root the sender names, since that root is the sending relay's (D4).

## The agent's plans

The agent (`src/detent_delivery/coordinator.cc`) opens with `"pages":true` and takes a W6 pages frame two ways, neither running the packer:

- **A full copy** (`after` 0): `receive_image` names the generation as a full copy over the selected base (`next_generation(..., true)`), receives over the empty base with `transfer_segment_ids` (the segment builder's ids, from ordinal 0) and returns the build's image: its packs are byte for byte the ones a records build of the same state writes. `PagedSnapshotPlanBuilder` takes that image in place of the records encoder.
- **A delta** (`after` the store's position): `receive_delta` over the selected base, whose pages are read with first touch, then `PagedTransferPlanBuilder`: the new packs are the received ones, named by `next_generation` over the base; a walk of the base from its root that stops at the pages the transfer reuses gives the removed base pages per pack and the new root's counts, and `SparsePlanFinalizer`'s warm validator must find the same removal.

Both name the frame's namespace, and a delta's plan refuses a tree that does not keep exactly one watermark. A refusal of either fails the response; the next open lists the namespace in `"records"` until records publish it (D9). A base that lacks a reused page, fails first touch, or does not take the delta asks a full copy (namespace D10.3).

A response cut inside a pages frame keeps the transfer's whole 64 KiB chunks; the next W5 next names them in `"resume"`, and a frame for the same root and `after` starting at that offset continues them (D8). A records frame that names a root must build to it: a build that does not counts `transfers.diverged` and asks a full copy (D1).

## The relay's builds

The relay (`src/detent_relay/namespace_relay.cc`, `page_copy.cc`, `page_window.cc`) answers a session that opened with `"pages":true` from generations it builds from the records it mirrors (D7):

- **Builds.** The first is a full build of the records full copy, named as the agent names a full copy, so its files are canonical. Each later head is built over the previous one from the records after it (dense-pages D7): `DeltaLeaves` (its one-namespace form, the watermark at the base's position) through `TrieOverlay` and `OverlayAssembler` on the head's in-memory segments. The new generation holds the base segments its tree still reaches and the new ones, so generations share every segment they both reach; its root equals a full build's. A base the records no longer reach (below the cut, another incarnation) gets a full build.
- **The page window** holds the generations at up to 64 positions. Its bound counts real memory: the bytes of the segments the window holds that the head does not reach, each counted once, at most the head's page bytes; the oldest go first.
- **The canonical copy** (D7.4) is one full copy per namespace at s, kept as its transfer. A new head is relocated into seal order by DD2's `canonical_copy`, and becomes both the new copy and the head, when the delta from s is no smaller than a fresh copy, when s left the window, or when the head's segments hold more dead bytes than live ones. A relocation writes the files a full build at that position writes.
- **A full copy** goes as the canonical copy while the history still answers a delta from s; a cold reader then takes the D6 answer from s. A repair (D139) takes the copy only when s is at or above the position it names, and records at the head otherwise. Asking it starts the build at the head; with no copy yet the position gets no frame, as a Current answer does, while a build is in flight, and records when the build failed.
- **A delta** from a base the window holds goes as pages when D6 finds them smaller against the records answer from that base. The choice and the transfer are made once per base and head and cached (D2), within the head's page bytes. Below the head the delta waits for the build at the head, but only when D6 rule 1 could pick pages: when the records answer is at least a third of the head's root page, which every delta carries one of.
- **Answers** own every copy, transfer and generation they name until the body is encoded, so a build that lands during the answer cannot free them. A reader that holds a prefix of the transfer the relay answers with (`resume`: its root, `after` and offset) gets it from that offset (D8). A records frame names the root of the relay's generation at the head when it holds one (D1).

`MirrorService` runs one build at a time on a second thread. The mirror thread reads the files the records need and copies the layout and ring; decoding, the records encode, the build and a relocation run on the builder, and a build that lands wakes the mirror thread to answer the sessions that wait for it. At 100K keys a records answer goes in 2 ms while the cold build runs. `/v1/health` reports `mirror.pages`: builds (`incremental` of them over the previous head), `relocations`, build failures, and full copies and deltas sent as pages.

## Cost

A full copy hashes each page once and each segment once: at 5M, 306,989,416 page bytes and 315,759,038 pack bytes, as the dense build. `checked_hash_counts()` counts both.

## Tests

- `test_pages_coordinator` runs an agent against a test server that sends DD1 transfers: a 100K-key cold agent boots from pages with no build hash, its descriptor and packs equal to the server's build; a clustered change of 10,000 keys comes as a delta transfer (251 KB against 2.84 MB for the full copy) and publishes with no build hash; a wrong root, for a full copy and for a delta, falls back to records; a full copy or delta holding another namespace's leaf is refused and falls back to records; the cold boot's first response, cut halfway, resumes at a 64 KiB boundary; a records frame naming a root its build does not reach is refused and the namespace repaired; a repair while the source's newest build is two positions below the agent's skips a copy below its position from a source that ignores it, gets nothing from one that honors it until its head reaches it, and ends above it (D139). `test_namespace_coordinator` drives the same floor through a D136-failed entry, which the store no longer guards.
- `test_relay_pages` runs the relay over a scripted upstream that serves real history files: the full copy reproduces the relay's build under the agent's derived ids; heads are built over the previous one and the copy stays at s, D6 picks records for 5 scattered keys and pages for a clustered rewrite of 36,000 of 45,000, a base outside the window keeps records, and a rewrite of every key relocates the head into a new copy equal to a full build's; a build over a base has a full build's root through updates, deletes and re-inserts, and its relocation a full build's files; a frame keeps the copy it names while a build that lands mid-answer replaces it; a resumed reader gets the transfer's tail, and records name the head's root only from a generation at the head; the mirror refuses a pages frame from its upstream, and in the service a records answer goes while a 100K build runs (`mutants/build-on-mirror-thread.patch` builds inline and fails it).
- `test_page_window` checks the Merkle diff against the oracle, and the window's bounds: positions, and the bytes of the segments only older generations reach, each shared segment counted once, through a rebase.
- `test_system_relay_pages` runs three authority voters, a relay and two agents, one on the authority (records) and one on the relay: the relayed agent boots 100K keys from one page transfer with zero bytes hashed to build, to the records agent's root, and a clustered rewrite of 55,555 keys made while the relay is paused reaches it as one pages delta (1.2 MB). `forcing/relay_pages_forced.sh` under `forcing/relay-root-forced.patch` makes every full copy name a wrong root: the agent refuses it and boots from records, to the same root, and its session then takes the delta as records.
- `test_local_paged_transfer` builds generations with the packer and checks the round trip (pages equal outside placement, the re-made transfer of the received generation equal to the stream, the hash counters), a delta over a base, a refusal per check, a foreign leaf or a missing watermark in a full copy and a delta, and a tree packed against D5 under a recomputed root.
- `test_local_paged_transfer --fixtures DIR [--only SLUG]` replays the model's fixtures (`dense-distribution-model.py check|full|delta CORPUS --out=DIR`): the full copy's root, packs byte for byte with the model's segment ids and the hash counters; the deltas' roots; and each refusal case of D4's table.
- The fixtures come from a corpus in today's definition encoding (`corpus-100k-varint.bin`, SHA-256 `39a0751b…2acf821`): the design's r12 corpora (`bb87ae5d…`, `3214561e…`) store definitions in an older body encoding, which the leaf check refuses. On that corpus largest-first packs as D5 does, so the model forges the non-canonical tree densest-first.
- `tests/detent_testing/paged_transfer_fuzz.cc` damages honest full and delta transfers: an accepted transfer must equal the honest result byte for byte. `test_testing_paged_transfer_fuzz_replay` replays seeded inputs; `-DDETENT_TRANSFER_FUZZ=ON` builds the libFuzzer binary, and `tests/detent_testing/transfer_fuzz.sh` builds it with brew LLVM and fuzzes it for one CPU-hour.
